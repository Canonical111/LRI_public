# One central-binomial series for the remaining C6 wheel

## Result and scope

Input: `inputs/previous_expression.json`, the supplied
`C6_wheel_hierarchy_expression.json`. All weights are in
`r6 = 2187 C6(1)` units.

The wheel can be written with one central-binomial coefficient sequence instead
of two differently shifted gamma-ratio sequences. The new positive summand is
linear in second-order harmonic sums and contains only pi and rational numbers.
In the full known part, every reciprocal power of Q cancels, as does the
standalone pi cubed term generated by the earlier wheel subtractions.

**Three auxiliary quantities remain.** This does not evaluate the entire wheel
in finitely many ordinary hypergeometric values. Literal direct convergence is
slower: the new summands are O(n^-4), versus O(n^-10) for W10. The old certified
hierarchy remains a valid alternative. A separate rational evaluator for the
new series, using pi as its only initial transcendental constant, is also
constructed and certified here.

The analytic selection identity used in this calculation is a consequence of
Mishev's completed-L invariance [1]. Its explicit second-order form is recorded
in Bao's Proposition 5.4 [2]. It is not claimed as a newly discovered theorem.
The exact eigenpath calculation below rederives that consequence, and its
application to the retained wheel and the polynomial compensation are checked
in `verify.py`.

The project-specific quartic periodization, contour normalizations, source
repair, and upstream graph/RG identification remain inherited inputs. This is
not a new certification of the complete physical C6 coefficient.

 H_n^{(r)}=\sum_{j=1}^n j^{-r},\quad H_0^{(r)}=0.
\]

\[
 \boxed{W_{\rm central}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty
 a_n^4\left[8+5\pi(4n+1)a_n^2\right]\delta_n.}                 \tag{1}

\[

\[
 a_{n+1}=a_n\frac{2n+1}{2n+2},\qquad
 \delta_{n+1}=\delta_n-\frac4{(2n+1)^2}+\frac1{(n+1)^2}.

There is no variable-argument special-function call, product of harmonic sums,

## 2. The core harmonic identity

Put ell=log(2),
\[
 h_n=\ell+H_n-H_{2n},\qquad
 \tau_n=12\zeta(3)-16H_{2n}^{(3)}+4H_n^{(3)},
 \qquad q_n=(4n+1)a_n^6.
\]
The identity needed for the wheel is
\[
 \boxed{\sum_{n\ge0}a_n^4(8h_n\delta_n+\tau_n)
 =\pi^2\sum_{n\ge0}q_n\delta_n.}                               \tag{5}
\]

### 2.1 A vanishing second coefficient of an ordinary 7F6

Define
\[
 A_n=4H_{2n}-3H_n-2\ell+\frac1{4n+1},\qquad
 R_n=4H_{2n}^{(2)}-H_n^{(2)}+\frac2{(4n+1)^2}.
\]
The symmetry consequence is
\[
 \boxed{\sum_{n\ge0}q_n\left(A_n^2-\frac12R_n\right)=0.}       \tag{6}
\]
It appears as Proposition 5.4 of [2]. For completeness the exact derivation from
[1] is specified here; this is also the construction replayed by `verify.py`.

Let L(a,b,c,d;e;f,g) be Mishev's completed function on
\[
 e+f+g-a-b-c-d=1.
\]
For alpha=d+g-e and Re(f-d)>0, its ordinary hypergeometric representation is
\[
 L=\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}
 {\pi\Gamma(g)\Gamma(1+g-e)\Gamma(f-d)
  \Gamma(1+a+d-e)\Gamma(1+b+d-e)\Gamma(1+c+d-e)}
\]
\[
 \qquad\times{}_7F_6\left(
 \begin{matrix}\alpha,1+\alpha/2,g-a,g-b,g-c,d,1+d-e\\
 \alpha/2,1+a+d-e,1+b+d-e,1+c+d-e,1+g-e,g
 \end{matrix};1\right).
                                                               \tag{7}
\]
The external input is its invariance under
\[
 \mathcal C(a,b,c,d;e;f,g)
 =(g-c,a,b,g-d;1+a+b-f;g,1+a+b-e).
                                                               \tag{8}
\]
This is [1, Proposition 3.2] followed by allowed permutations.

Let omega=exp(pi i/4), and set
\[
 p_0=(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2;1;1,1),
\]
\[
 v=(\omega^2,\omega,1,1-\omega+\omega^2;
       1+\omega^2;\omega^2-\omega^3,1+\omega^3).
\]
Direct algebra gives
\[
 \mathcal C(p_0+xv)=p_0+\omega xv.
\]
Thus L(p0+xv)=L(p0+omega xv), and its quadratic coefficient is zero, since
omega^2 is not one. The 7F6 excess is one at the base point; it stays positive
on a small closed neighborhood, permitting termwise differentiation twice.
The differentiated terms have summable bounds of order
n^(-2+epsilon)(1+log^2 n), for some epsilon<1.

Write rho=1-omega+omega^3=1-sqrt(2). Include the gamma prefactor in each
summand l_n(x) of (7). Its base value is l_n(0)=q_n/(2 pi).
After separating the very-well-poised factor alpha+2n, the sums of parameter
slopes needed for the gamma differentiation are:

| Quantity | Sum of slopes | Sum of squares of slopes |
|---|---:|---:|
| six half-parameter gamma factors depending on n | 4 rho | 2 rho^2 |
| five unit-parameter denominator gamma factors depending on n | 2 rho | 0 |
| half-parameter normalization factors | 2 rho | 2 rho^2 |

The linear-factor slope is rho. Gamma/polygamma recurrences [3] give
\[
 (\log l_n)'(0)=2\rho A_n,\qquad
 (\log l_n)''(0)=-2\rho^2R_n.
\]
Consequently
\[
 l_n''(0)=\frac{q_n}{2\pi}4\rho^2(A_n^2-R_n/2).
\]
Summation proves (6). Every fixed-point, eigenvector and slope identity is
checked exactly in Q[omega]/(omega^4+1); no approximate eigenvector is used.

### 2.2 Apply that coefficient identity to the project wheel

Use the already defined
\[
 L_n=8H_{2n}-6H_n-4\ell+\frac2{4n+1}=2A_n,
\]
\[
 M_n=\frac{5\pi^2}{3}-16H_{2n}^{(2)}+6H_n^{(2)}
       -\frac4{(4n+1)^2},\qquad
 S_7=\sum q_n(L_n^2+M_n).
\]
The pointwise polynomial identity
\[
 L_n^2+M_n-(\pi^2+2\delta_n)=4(A_n^2-R_n/2)
\]
therefore gives
\[
 S_7=\pi^2 V+2D_6,\qquad V=\sum q_n,\quad D_6=\sum q_n\delta_n.
                                                               \tag{9}
\]
Two project-specific analytic identities are retained in the input derivations:
\[
 S_7=\frac{3B_s}{2\pi^2}-\frac{32}{\pi^2}H_*,\qquad
 H_*=\sum a_n^4h_n(8h_n^2+\delta_n),
\]
\[
 \sum a_n^4(128h_n^3+24h_n\delta_n+\tau_n)=B_s/2,
 \qquad B_s=2\pi^4V.                                         \tag{10}
\]
The last normalization in (10) follows from the inherited zeroth moment
I(1/2)=pi^2 V/2=B_s/(4pi^2). Subtracting 16H* from the cubic combination and
applying (9)-(10) gives
\[
 \sum a_n^4(8h_n\delta_n+\tau_n)
 =\frac{\pi^2S_7}{2}-\frac{B_s}{4}=\pi^2D_6,
\]
which proves (5). These are ordinary absolutely convergent sums; there is no
subtraction of divergent series.

The inherited positive wheel is
\[
 W_+=\sum a_n^4[8\pi\delta_n+5(8h_n\delta_n+\tau_n)].
\]
Equation (5) yields
\[
 \boxed{W_+=\pi\left[\frac{(8+5\pi)\pi^2}{3}+W_{\rm central}\right].}
                                                               \tag{11}
\]
The term separated here is exactly the n=0 summand. Combining (11) with the
saved rational-counterterm relations proves (2); `verify.py` checks this via
both the compact formula and the full legacy chain.

## 3. Direct positivity and convergence

For x=n+1/4, the polygamma integral [3] gives
\[
 \delta_n=\frac12\int_0^\infty u e^{-xu}\operatorname{sech}(u/4)\,du,
 \qquad 0<\delta_n<\frac1{2x^2}.
\]
The elementary induction bound a_n^2<=1/(3n+1) implies a_n^2<=1/(3x) and
(4n+1)a_n^2<=4/3. Hence (1) converges, and for terms 1<=n<N,
\[
 \boxed{0<W_{\rm central}-W_{{\rm central},N}
 <\frac{8+20\pi/3}{54(N-3/4)^3},\qquad N\ge1.}                 \tag{12}
\]
The actual asymptotic summand is 14/(pi^2 n^4)+O(n^-5), by gamma-ratio
asymptotics [4]. This is slower than the old W10 summand. Its advantage is a
single coefficient stream and removal of Q and G from the series and of
reciprocal Q from the known polynomial.

## 4. A separate exact-rational cubic-in-pi evaluator

The hierarchy used here corrects the infinite tail only; it does not change (4)
or move another constant into its known part. This section specifies the error
bound used to certify the new series independently of the old wheel intervals.

Define Delta_r q(t)=q(t)-[(t+1/2)/(t+1)]^r q(t+1), x=t+1/4,
w=1/x and y=w^2. Let
\[
 d_j=\frac{E_{2j}(2j+1)}{2\,16^j},\qquad
 \operatorname{sech}v=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{E_{2j}v^{2j}}{(2j)!}.
\]

### 4.1 Fourth-power corrections

Use the retained exact construction
\[
 \phi_{-1}^{(4)}(t)=\frac{(t-1/4)^3}{(t-1/2)^4},\qquad
 \phi_j^{(4)}(t)=\frac{t-1/4}{t^j(t-1/2)^{j+4}},\quad j\ge0.
\]
Choose rational alpha_j successively so that
\[
 q_M^{(4)}=\sum_{j=-1}^M\alpha_j\phi_j^{(4)},\qquad
 \Delta_4q_M^{(4)}=\sum_{k=0}^{M+1}d_k y^{k+1}+O(y^{M+3}).
\]
The leading triangular multipliers are 3 for j=-1 and 2j+5 for j>=0,
so this specification determines every coefficient uniquely.
The initial values are alpha_-1=1/6, alpha_0=-77/480.

Let
\[
 D_4(y)=(1-y/16)^M(1-9y/16)^{M+4},\quad
 \Delta_4q_M^{(4)}=P_4(y)/D_4(y),
\]
\[
 U_4(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{M+1}d_k y^{k+1},\qquad
 E_4(y)=\frac{P_4(y)-D_4(y)U_4(y)}{y^{M+3}}.
\]
P4 and E4 are polynomials, computed by exact rational operations. For
Y=(N+1/4)^(-2), define
\[
 K_4=|d_{M+2}|+\frac{\sum_j|[y^j]E_4(y)|Y^j}{D_4(Y)}.
\]
Then |delta(t)-Delta4 q4_M(t)|<=K4 x^(-2M-6) for t>=N.

### 4.2 Sixth-power corrections: new construction

The second stream is a_n^6 (4n+1) delta_n. Use
\[
 \phi_j^{(6)}(t)=\frac{(t-1/4)^6}{t^j(t-1/2)^{j+6}},\quad j\ge0,
 \qquad q_L^{(6)}=\sum_{j=0}^L\beta_j\phi_j^{(6)}.
\]
With
\[
 F_j(w)=\frac{(1-w/2)^6}{(1-w/4)^j(1-3w/4)^{j+6}},
\]
exact rational reflection gives
\[
 \Delta_6\phi_j^{(6)}=w^{2j}[F_j(w)-F_j(-w)].
\]
Its leading term is (2j+3)w^(2j+1). Choose beta_j successively to match
4x delta(t), namely
\[
 \Delta_6q_L^{(6)}=4w\sum_{k=0}^L d_k y^k+O(wy^{L+1}).
\]
For example,
\[
 \beta_0=\frac23,\quad\beta_1=-\frac{91}{120},\quad
 \beta_2=\frac{1797}{2240},\quad\beta_3=-\frac{375337}{322560}.
\]
Let
\[
 D_6(y)=(1-y/16)^L(1-9y/16)^{L+6},\quad
 \Delta_6q_L^{(6)}=wP_6(y)/D_6(y),
\]
\[
 U_6(y)=4\sum_{k=0}^L d_k y^k,\qquad
 E_6(y)=\frac{P_6(y)-D_6(y)U_6(y)}{y^{L+1}}.
\]
Again these are polynomials, not infinite expansions. Define
\[
 K_6=4|d_{L+1}|+\frac{\sum_j|[y^j]E_6(y)|Y^j}{D_6(Y)}.
\]
Then |4x delta(t)-Delta6 q6_L(t)|<=K6 x^(-2L-3).
Both denominator polynomials are positive for 0<=y<=Y with N>=2.

### 4.3 The finite Taylor estimate is global

The preceding bounds do not assume convergence of an asymptotic series.
The product formula [5]
\[
 \cosh v=\prod_{k\ge1}\left(1+rac{4v^2}{(2k-1)^2\pi^2}\right)
\]
shows that sech(sqrt(s)) is a Laplace transform of a probability measure on
[0,infinity). One can construct the measure as the law of a sum of independent
exponential variables whose means are the product's positive coefficients;
the sum of the means is finite. Applying the finite Taylor remainder for the
exponential inside that representation gives, for every finite K and real v,
\[
 \left|\operatorname{sech}v-\sum_{k=0}^K\frac{E_{2k}v^{2k}}{(2k)!}\right|
 \le\frac{|E_{2K+2}|v^{2K+2}}{(2K+2)!}.
\]
Integrating this bound in the displayed Laplace representation of delta gives
the next-term error estimates used in K4 and K6. At fixed t, increasing M
indefinitely is not asserted to converge; the explicit finite bound controls
each chosen pair (N,M).

### 4.4 Complete finite approximant and bound

Take L=M+1, and form
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W_{N,M}={}&\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}
 a_n^4[8+5\pi(4n+1)a_n^2]\delta_n\cr
 &+8a_N^4q_M^{(4)}(N)+5\pi a_N^6q_{M+1}^{(6)}(N).
 \end{aligned}                                                 \tag{13}
\]
The added terms evaluate the two rational tails completely by telescoping.
The upper boundaries vanish: q4 is O(t^-1) and q6 is O(1), whereas the
respective gamma weights are O(t^-2) and O(t^-3).

Using a_n^4<=1/(9x^2), a_n^6<=1/(27x^3), pi<22/7 and integral comparison gives
\[
 \boxed{|W_{\rm central}-W_{N,M}|
 \le\frac{(8/9)K_4+(110/189)K_6}
 {(2M+7)(N-3/4)^{2M+7}}.}                                     \tag{14}
\]
Every finite approximant is exactly a rational cubic in pi:
\[
 W_{N,M}=A_{N,M}\pi^3+B_{N,M}\pi^2+C_{N,M}\pi+D_{N,M}.
\]
The certificate encloses pi using Machin's formula and alternating rational
arctangent series. Rational interval arithmetic then encloses this cubic,
adds (14), and rounds the final endpoints outward to rational dyadics.
No Catalan/gamma value, old wheel interval, quadrature, or numerical reference
is used to construct these intervals.
