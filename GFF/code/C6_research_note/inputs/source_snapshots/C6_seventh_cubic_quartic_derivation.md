# C6(1): the seventh-sector parameters need only two cubics and one quartic

## Result, source, and limitations

The accepted input is `inputs/previous_expression.json`, copied from
`C6_seventh_Abel_expression.json`. It contains twelve fixed function values.
Its five seventh-sector values each use ten roots of a degree-ten polynomial.
The identification of that sector with C6, its weights, the known polynomial,
the source repair and all previous graph/RG qualifications are inherited.

The new calculation replaces those five functions by four
`F_{14:0;3}^{14:1;4}` values and one `F_{16:0;3}^{16:1;4}` value. Thirty-two
root parameters are generated by just ten polynomial roots: the roots of TWO
cubics and ONE quartic, followed by square roots and explicit shifts.
All new lower parameters have strictly positive real parts. No additional
special-function class, infinite sum, integral, derivative, or limit is retained
in the coefficient. The twelve-value count and the known polynomial do not change.

This is a simplification of the finite parameter specification, not a new
arithmetic-independence relation, evaluation in elementary constants, or a
proof of optimality. Literal convergence remains algebraic, of the same order
as in the immediately preceding Abel representation. Two local upper lists
include a negative nonintegral parameter, so the earlier positive-coefficient
claim is not retained for those local lists.

## 1. Explicit parameters

Put
\[
A=\pi+4\log2,\quad
\xi_+=-\frac{55A}{98}+\frac{(401+735\sqrt2)\pi}{1598},\quad
\xi_-=-\frac{55A}{98}+\frac{(401-735\sqrt2)\pi}{1598}.
\]
The inherited five weights are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
r&p_r&\xi_r\\\hline
1&\cos^7(\pi/8)(13+22\sqrt2)&\xi_+\\
2&-35&37A/196-67\pi/140\\
3&\cos^7(\pi/8)(22\sqrt2-13)&\xi_-+4\\
5&-\sin^7(\pi/8)(22\sqrt2-13)&\xi_-+4/3\\
7&\sin^7(\pi/8)(13+22\sqrt2)&\xi_++24/5
\end{array}
\]
with \(\xi_r=b_r/(98p_r)\). Define only three new elementary constants:
\[
\mu_+=\xi_++4,\qquad\mu_-=\xi_-,\qquad
\mu_0=1+\frac{37A}{196}-\frac{67\pi}{140}.
\]
Their approximate values are 3.51266739683952846, -4.57434006191691609,
and 0.61297612244373291, respectively. They are computed from known constants,
not fitted to a numerical period.

Define the cubic and quartic
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_\mu(z)=4096\mu z^3+(3072-768\mu)z^2
 +(640-720\mu)z+12-17\mu,}
\tag{1}
\]
\[
\boxed{\mathcal D_\mu(z)=65536\mu z^4+(65536-49152\mu)z^3
 +(28672-53760\mu)z^2+(1792-4032\mu)z+16-39\mu.}
\tag{2}
\]
For each r, write
\[
t=r/8,\qquad a=t+1/4,\quad b=t+3/4,\quad c=t+1/2.
\]
For r=1,7 take all three roots of \(\mathcal C_{\mu_+}\); for r=3,5 take
all three roots of \(\mathcal C_{\mu_-}\). In each case form the SIX-member list
\[
\boldsymbol\theta_r=(c+\sqrt{z_1},c-\sqrt{z_1},\ldots,
 c+\sqrt{z_3},c-\sqrt{z_3}).\tag{3}
\]
For r=2, use all four roots of \(\mathcal D_{\mu_0}\) and form the analogous
EIGHT-member list. Both signs are included, so square-root branch choice and
root ordering do not affect the function. These theta parameters are not the
wheel's harmonic remainder delta_n.

No root problem above degree four remains. The resulting parameters need not
have algebraic degree at most four, because a square root is subsequently taken.
They are algebraic over the field generated by pi, log(2), and sqrt(2), not
asserted algebraic over the rationals.

## 2. Five simpler Kampé de Fériet values

Use the classical complex-variable convention [1]: the shared lists act on
m+n, the next lists on m and the last on n, with the usual factorial factors.
A repeated-list exponent [k] denotes k entries, and 1+theta means componentwise
addition. An empty list is denoted by a dash.

For r in {1,3,5,7}, set mu_r=mu_+ for r=1,7 and mu_r=mu_- for r=3,5. Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal K_r^{\rm sym}=
F_{14:0;3}^{14:1;4}\!\left[
\begin{matrix}
a^{[6]},a+1,c+1,1+\boldsymbol\theta_r:1;1,t+1,a,b-1\\
b,(b+1)^{[6]},c,\boldsymbol\theta_r:-;t,a+1,b
\end{matrix};-1,-1\right].}\tag{4}
\]
Its multiplier is
\[
\boxed{\Omega_r^{\rm sym}=
\left[\frac{\Gamma(a)}{\Gamma(b)}\right]^7
\frac{tc\,\mathcal C_{\mu_r}(c^2)}{2048(b-1)b^6}.}\tag{5}
\]
For r=2, use t=1/4,a=1/2,b=1,c=3/4 and the eight-member theta list:
\[
\boxed{\mathcal K_2^{\rm sym}=
F_{16:0;3}^{16:1;4}\!\left[
\begin{matrix}
a^{[7]},c+1,1+\boldsymbol\theta_2:1;1,t+1,a-1,b\\
(b+1)^{[7]},c,\boldsymbol\theta_2:-;t,a,b+1
\end{matrix};-1,-1\right].}\tag{6}
\]
Its multiplier simplifies to
\[
\boxed{\Omega_2^{\rm sym}=\frac{3\pi^{7/2}}{1024}(84\mu_0-85).}\tag{7}
\]
Equivalently it is
\[
[\Gamma(a)/\Gamma(b)]^7\frac{tc\,\mathcal D_{\mu_0}(c^2)}
 {32768(a-1)b^8}.
\]
The separate r=2 formula avoids the singular b-1=0 that would result from
naively using (4) at this shift. No parameter limit or regularization is used.

The exact full-sector replacement is
\[
\boxed{B_7=9408\pi^{3/2}\sum_{r\in\{1,2,3,5,7\}}
 p_r\Omega_r^{\rm sym}\mathcal K_r^{\rm sym}.}\tag{8}
\]
Only five terms occur. For each shift,
\[
\Omega_r^{\rm sym}\mathcal K_r^{\rm sym}
=\widehat\Omega_r\widehat{\mathcal K}_r,
\]
where the right-hand side is the preceding degree-ten Abel representation.

## 3. Proof by one contiguous shift and finite summation by parts

All sums in the proof are intermediate; the coefficient (8) uses fixed
function values. Start from the precise inherited target
\[
T_r=\sum_{n\ge0}u_n(H_n+\xi_r),\quad
u_n=(-1)^n(n+t)[\Gamma(n+a)/\Gamma(n+b)]^7,
\]
where
\[
H_n=\frac12\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac1{j+a}+\frac1{j+b}\right).
\]
The full sector is 9408 pi^(3/2) times the finite weighted sum of these T_r.

Choose
\[
(s,q)=\begin{cases}(b-1,a),&r=1,3,5,7,\\(a-1,b),&r=2.\end{cases}
\]
In both cases s+q=2t and s is nonzero. Put
\[
\bar h(n)=\frac12\left(\frac1{n+s}+\frac1{n+q}\right)
=\frac{n+t}{(n+s)(n+q)},\quad
\bar H_n=\sum_{j<n}\bar h(j),\quad
\mu=\xi_r-\frac1{2s}.
\]
The finite harmonic identity is
\[
\boxed{\bar H_n=H_n+\frac1{2s}-\frac1{2(n+s)}.}\tag{9}
\]
The choice of s makes mu equal to mu_+ for r=1,7, mu_- for r=3,5, and mu_0 for r=2.
This is why two different pairs of functions share their cubic roots.

Let
\[
V_n=(-1)^n[\Gamma(n+a)/\Gamma(n+b)]^7
 (n+s)\left[\mu(n+q)-\frac12\right],\qquad
w_n=u_n+V_n-V_{n+1}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
V_n=u_n\left[\frac{\mu+1/(2(n+s))}{\bar h(n)}-1\right].
\]
For every positive integer N, finite summation by parts gives
\[
\boxed{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}u_n(H_n+\xi_r)
 -\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}w_n\bar H_{n+1}=V_N\bar H_N.}\tag{10}
\]
The lower boundary vanishes because bar H_0=0, despite the possible negative
first increment. The original n=0 contribution is present in this identity.
Gamma recurrence [2] gives
\[
u_{n+1}/u_n=-\frac{n+t+1}{n+t}\left(\frac{n+a}{n+b}\right)^7.
\]
The gamma-ratio asymptotic [3] implies V_N=O(N^(-3/2)) and
bar H_N=log N+O(1). Hence the right side of (10) tends to zero.

### The odd polynomial is the source of the simplification

For r!=2, direct substitution yields
\[
\boxed{w_n=(-1)^n[\Gamma(n+a)/\Gamma(n+b)]^7
\frac{(n+a)(n+c)\mathcal C_\mu((n+c)^2)}{2048(n+b)^6}.}\tag{11}
\]
To see the reflection, write y=n+c. The polynomial before extracting y is
\[
(y+\tfrac14)^6[\mu(y-\tfrac34)+\tfrac12]
+(y-\tfrac14)^6[\mu(y+\tfrac34)-\tfrac12]
=\frac{y}{2048}\mathcal C_\mu(y^2).
\tag{12}
\]
It is odd in y; the remaining factor is a cubic in y^2.

For r=2 the corresponding identity is
\[
\boxed{w_n=(-1)^n[\Gamma(n+a)/\Gamma(n+b)]^7
\frac{(n+c)\mathcal D_{\mu_0}((n+c)^2)}{32768(n+b)^7}.}\tag{13}
\]
Here the finite polynomial is
\[
(y+\tfrac14)^8[\mu(y-\tfrac54)+\tfrac12]
+(y-\tfrac14)^8[\mu(y+\tfrac54)-\tfrac12]
=\frac{y}{32768}\mathcal D_\mu(y^2).
\tag{14}
\]
It is again odd, leaving a quartic in y^2.

Factorization in the lists (3) gives
\[
\frac{\mathcal C_\mu((n+c)^2)}{\mathcal C_\mu(c^2)}
=\prod_{\theta\in\boldsymbol\theta_r}
 \frac{(1+\theta)_n}{(\theta)_n},
\]
and similarly for the quartic. The rest of (11) gives the shared ratio
\[
\frac{(a)_n^6(a+1)_n(c+1)_n}{(b)_n(b+1)_n^6(c)_n};
\]
the rest of (13) gives
\[
\frac{(a)_n^7(c+1)_n}{(b+1)_n^7(c)_n}.
\]
The local coefficient is
\[
\frac{\bar h(j)}{\bar h(0)}=
\frac{(t+1)_j(s)_j(q)_j}{(t)_j(s+1)_j(q+1)_j}.
\]
Reindexing the triangle in w_n bar H_(n+1) by n=m+j now gives exactly (4) or
(6), with multiplier bar h(0)w_0. That multiplier is (5) or (7), proving (8).

## 4. Exact parameter admissibility

The three polynomials have the following disjoint rational isolating intervals
for ALL their roots:

| Polynomial | Negative roots | Positive root |
|---|---|---|
| C_mu+ | (-.364,-.362), (-.026,-.025) | (.361,.363) |
| C_mu- | (-.299,-.298), (-.024,-.023) | (.673,.675) |
| D_mu0 | (-.974,-.972), (-.085,-.083), (-.013,-.012) | (.188,.190) |

The decimals in this table are exact terminating rational endpoints. Exact
rational interval evaluations give opposite signs at both ends of every bracket.
Each list supplies as many disjoint sign changes as the polynomial's degree,
so all roots are real, simple, and accounted for.

The largest possible positive root is below the smallest c_r^2 in its group:
.363 < (5/8)^2, .675 < (7/8)^2, and .190 < (3/4)^2. Thus both c +/- sqrt(z)
have positive real parts. Negative roots produce conjugate pairs with real
part c>0. All other lower parameters in (4) and (6) are positive rationals.
No denominator parameter is zero or a negative integer.

The only negative numerator parameters are b-1=-1/8 for r=1 and a-1=-1/2 for
r=2. They are nonintegral and cause no parameter singularity. They do cause
signed local coefficients, so no positivity claim is made for those series.

The certificate bounds pi by the alternating Machin formula, log(2) by the
positive atanh(1/3) series with a geometric remainder, and sqrt(2) by integer
square roots. It certifies the parameter statements only, not numerical values
of the special functions or of C6.

## 5. Convergence, unchanged sectors and executed checks

The new signed shared coefficient obeys
w_n=2 mu_r (-1)^n n^(-3/2)(1+O(1/n)). The inner absolute local sum is O(log n),
even in the two signed cases. The total-index diagonal is therefore absolutely
summable with order O(log(n+2)/(n+1)^(3/2)). This is the SAME algebraic order as
the previous degree-ten construction, not a promised speed improvement.

The known polynomial, source correction, wheel, primitive, three Meijer G
blocks and two polylogarithmic values are preserved exactly. No new zero-index
compensation is introduced. The master formula and every unchanged parameter
list are reproduced in FORMULA.md and results/expression.json.

`build_and_verify.py` executes 173 finite checks: the contiguous harmonic
identity, finite Abel boundaries for all five shifts, gamma/Pochhammer ratios,
odd polynomial factorization, full old coefficient matching, rational root
isolation, list counts and preservation of unchanged fields. These are finite
implementation/algebra checks, not 173 independent analytic theorems.

`numerical.py` separately constructs the old shifted-harmonic coefficients,
the previous degree-ten Abel coefficients and the new factorized coefficients.
It also evaluates root-based Pochhammer lists at six separate indices per shift.
At N=400 Euler-weighted diagonals and 160 working decimal digits, the old/new
complete seventh-sector difference is approximately 3.619e-113 in r6 units.
The largest relative root-based coefficient discrepancy is below 2.668e-160.
A separate N=240,105-digit run is included. These are FLOATING-POINT checks,
not a new rigorous truncation or special-value interval certificate.

No new all-sector numerical evaluation or independent graph/RG proof was
performed. Root isolation does not certify the accuracy of the numerical
Euler approximations. No Mathematica kernel execution is claimed.
